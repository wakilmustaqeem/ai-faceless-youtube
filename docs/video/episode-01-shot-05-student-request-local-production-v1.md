# Episode 01 — Shot 05: Student Request — Local Production Lock v1

## Purpose
Create the first human-to-human participation turn before the steering bug.

## Duration
Target 7–12 seconds.

## Action
1. The first test car continues moving on the right display.
2. Students track the car naturally.
3. One student raises a hand after watching the first lap begin.
4. Presenter notices, stops his own movement, and gives the student attention.
5. Student asks: “Sir… can we actually race it?”
6. Presenter gives a small confident smile and replies: “Let’s find out.”
7. Cut/transition toward the permission-control moment that follows.

## Dialogue
Student: “Sir… can we actually race it?”
Presenter: “Let’s find out.”

## Human performance
- Student raises one hand naturally, not perfectly upright or synchronized.
- Presenter makes genuine eye contact before responding.
- Student lowers the hand naturally after being acknowledged.
- Nearby students show small individual reactions.
- Natural blinking, breathing, posture changes and eye focus.
- No exaggerated classroom acting.

## Continuity
- Same 2040 classroom.
- Same presenter, wardrobe and student group.
- Exactly three fixed physical LCDs.
- Same racing car, track and UI from Shot 04.
- Same master mobile control console.
- No floating screens or holograms.
- Maintain established lighting and camera geography.

## Camera
Medium classroom two-shot: presenter in foreground/center and student visible within the same physical space. Slow, subtle push-in during the question. Avoid dramatic zooms.

## Visual prompt
“Photorealistic live-action cinematic 2040 international technology classroom, same established full-body male teacher, same students and same three fixed wall-mounted displays, first racing-car test visibly running on the right display, one student naturally raises a hand and asks the teacher if they can actually race it, teacher turns toward the student with authentic eye contact and a small confident smile, realistic classroom behavior, individual student reactions, natural breathing and blinking, believable human anatomy and weight, premium but restrained future-classroom lighting, realistic reflections, subtle interface glow, grounded physical technology, cinematic medium two-shot, emotionally warm and curious, no artificial avatar appearance.”

## Negative prompt
“cartoon, anime, avatar, mannequin, plastic skin, frozen faces, exaggerated acting, synchronized reactions, unnatural raised hand, deformed fingers, extra limbs, identity drift, floating displays, holographic screens, fantasy technology, horror, excessive neon, camera shake, extreme zoom, slideshow, watermark, text artifacts.”

## Audio
Use natural classroom room tone. Keep the student voice clear and conversational. Presenter response should sound calm and confident. Do not add a dramatic trailer sting.

## Acceptance gate
PASS only if:
- Student action reads as intentional and natural.
- Presenter visibly notices and responds to the student.
- Dialogue timing matches the visible mouth movement if generated with dialogue.
- Presenter/student identities remain stable.
- Three displays remain physically fixed.
- Racing-game continuity matches Shot 04.
- No avatar/slideshow aesthetic.
- Human owner approves before promotion to final assembly.
