# Time-Travel Teleportation — Cinematic Production Lock v1

## Canonical visual sequence

Every major application teleport follows the same recognizable sequence:

**YEAR SELECT → DESTINATION CONFIRM → LIGHT BURST → DISAPPEARANCE → SCREEN UPDATE → ARRIVAL LIGHT BURST → MATERIALIZATION → NEW WORLD / YEAR / ERA / LOCATION**

## Departure

- Traveler selects the destination year in the Time-Travel Application.
- Screen confirms the selected year.
- A bright, cinematic white/golden light rapidly builds around the traveler.
- The light peaks for a brief moment.
- Traveler disappears cleanly inside the light.
- Nearby environment remains physically consistent.
- No giant fantasy portal is required.
- The effect must feel like advanced technology, not magic.

## Transition

Use a short controlled transition:
- luminous particles;
- restrained glow;
- subtle spatial distortion;
- controlled motion blur;
- brief energy pulse;
- synchronized electronic whoosh/impact.

Avoid excessive particles or long portal sequences that hide the actor.

## Arrival

- A matching light burst appears at the destination.
- Light contracts toward the traveler's body.
- Traveler materializes with correct feet placement and natural balance.
- Breathing, blinking, clothing movement and environmental interaction begin immediately.
- Destination ambience is already present.
- No generic background or unexplained modern objects.

## Screen/UI behavior

During teleport:
**DESTINATION LOCKED**

Then:
**YEAR: 2027**
**ERA: PAST**

For a newly introduced destination/world:
**NEW WORLD**

Then resolve:
**YEAR: [year]**
**ERA: [PAST / PRESENT / FUTURE]**
**LOCATION: [location]**

“NEW WORLD” is a cinematic UI transition label. It does not automatically mean another planet.

## Sound design

Departure:
- short rising energy tone;
- light pulse;
- clean whoosh at disappearance.

Arrival:
- reverse/descending energy tone;
- brief impact;
- immediate destination ambience.

Do not use horror stings, alarms or fantasy magic sounds.

## Actor continuity

The same traveler must retain:
- face identity;
- body proportions;
- clothing continuity unless the Era Adaptation System intentionally changes wardrobe;
- accessories;
- carried approved objects;
- natural posture and movement.

No identity drift during the light burst or materialization.

## Camera

Prefer:
- medium/full-body framing for departure;
- enough negative space to read the complete light burst;
- matching composition or motivated camera movement at arrival;
- cinematic depth of field;
- stable controlled movement.

Do not hide teleportation with random cuts.

## Generation strategy

For local production, generate the actor/environment and teleport effect as controlled short shots where necessary, then assemble and composite locally. Do not claim a local generative renderer is ready until the production-machine preflight passes.

Reference-first generation remains the continuity priority.

## Acceptance gate

PASS only when:
- the year selection is readable;
- light burst clearly marks teleportation;
- disappearance and arrival are physically believable;
- screen updates match the selected destination;
- “NEW WORLD” appears only when the story introduces a new world/destination;
- traveler identity remains stable;
- materialization has natural footing and movement;
- no horror/magic/cartoon/avatar aesthetic appears;
- destination environment clearly belongs to the selected era;
- sound and visuals are synchronized;
- human owner approves the final shot.
